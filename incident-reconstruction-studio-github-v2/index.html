Six Structured Steps From Raw Scene Media to Deliverable Exhibit

Every part of the application follows a disciplined exhibit-building sequence designed to separate documented facts from assumptions and disputed details.

1

Project & Scene Upload

Set project title, incident type, case reference ID, preparer name, and scene notes. Load any authorized photograph, aerial map, or architectural plan as the base canvas.

2

Numbered Movement Routes

Create independently colored trajectories for vehicles, pedestrians, workers, or objects. Click or tap sequentially on the scene to plot numbered path waypoints.

3

Typed Scene Markers

Place editable markers for events, contact or interaction points, reported injury areas, evidence reference points, and areas of interest with high-contrast labels.

4

Event Chronology

Build a time-stamped or step-sequenced timeline tied to source records so reviewers can follow the progression alongside the spatial diagram.

5

Classified Evidence Register

Log photographs, videos, diagrams, measurements, witness statements, and reports tagged as Documented fact, Expert interpretation, Assumption, or Unresolved/disputed.

6

Export & Reopenable Projects

Export a high-resolution PNG exhibit, print a combined diagram-and-register layout, download a summary CSV, or save a reusable JSON project file.

Privacy Architecture

Your Scene Evidence Stays in Your Browser

Incident Reconstruction Studio operates client-side without uploading your photographs, diagrams, or case metadata to an external storage server. Project files remain in active browser memory until you choose to export them.
