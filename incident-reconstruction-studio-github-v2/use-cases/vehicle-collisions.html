Traffic & Roadway Exhibits

Vehicle Collision Scene Exhibits

Organize complex multi-vehicle roadway incidents into a clear, readable visual exhibit using authorized aerial imagery, police diagram scans, or site photographs.

Recommended Base Imagery

Start in Step 1 (Scene) by uploading a top-down aerial image, survey plan, or roadway diagram you have rights to use. Enter the intersection location, incident type, and case reference ID so the exhibit footer identifies the scene cleanly.

Mapping Vehicle Approaches with Numbered Routes

In Step 2 (Routes), assign a distinct color to each vehicle (for example, blue for Vehicle A and green for Vehicle B). Plot numbered waypoints along each lane of travel to illustrate pre-impact approach, evasive maneuvers, and post-contact paths.

Marking Impact, Debris & Reference Points

Use Step 3 (Markers) to place Contact / interaction markers at reported impact zones, Reference / evidence markers for skid marks, gouge marks, or debris fields, and Area of interest boxes for sightline obstructions or traffic control devices.

Pairing Spatial Layers with Chronology & Evidence

Record signal phase timing, dashcam timestamps, or witness sequence steps in Step 4 (Timeline). In Step 5 (Evidence), log crash reports, scene photos, and EDR or measurement records with explicit classifications separating documented facts from assumptions.

Exhibit Readiness Checklist

  • Route colors clearly differentiated per vehicle or road user
  • Contact/interaction marker labeled with its documentary source
  • Roadway reference landmarks (lanes, signs, crosswalks) visible in base imagery
  • Disputed speeds, lane positions, or signal states tagged as Unresolved / disputed
Scope Reminder: Incident Reconstruction Studio generates visual exhibits from user-entered data. It does not calculate speeds, braking distances, or collision forces, nor does it establish legal causation or medical diagnoses.
